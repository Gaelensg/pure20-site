Melanotan II

An unapproved, non-selective melanocortin agonist with small human studies showing pigmentation and erectile responses, unresolved long-term safety, and serious adverse-event reports.

What it is

Melanotan II, also called MT-2 or MT-II, is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide modeled on alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone, alpha-MSH. Its constrained structure produces activity across several melanocortin receptors. Module 4 placement and the goals Skin and Libido describe research interests, not established clinical indications.

Human development began with experimental pigmentation studies, followed by investigations of spontaneous erections and sexual desire. These outcomes require separate interpretation. Darker pigmentation does not establish healthier skin, and an erectile response does not establish a durable treatment for low desire. MT-2 has no approved prescribing label identified in the reviewed sources. There is no established benefit-risk profile or approved formulation for routine tanning or sexual-function treatment.

How it works

MT-2 stimulates multiple melanocortin receptors. The cited review emphasizes MC1R, MC3R and MC4R, with receptor activation increasing intracellular cyclic AMP. Non-selective activity does not mean equal effects across receptor subtypes or tissues. Receptor affinity alone cannot predict the balance between pigmentation, erectile responses and adverse effects in a particular person.

MC1R signaling promotes melanin production in melanocytes, providing a biological explanation for pigmentation changes. The three-person pigmentation pilot documented visible changes in two participants, PMID 8637402. It did not establish prevention of UV injury, skin cancer or photoaging. A pigmentation endpoint cannot substitute for clinical evidence of photoprotection. The trial also does not establish that stimulation of existing pigmented lesions has a favorable long-term risk profile.

Central MC4R signaling is implicated in erectile responses, while MC3R contributions remain incompletely resolved. Proposed interactions with oxytocinergic pathways remain mechanistic observations. These pathways are discussed in PMID 17584130. [King review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2694735/).

Rat experiments suggest central and peripheral neural involvement, depending on administration route, PMID 16360286. They do not support a simple direct relaxing effect on cavernosal smooth muscle. Appetite suppression occurred in human research, PMID 9679884, but the cited studies do not establish MT-2 as a weight-management treatment.

What the research says

Grade B reflects small, short human studies with physiological endpoints. Placebo-controlled crossover experiments provide preliminary human evidence but do not establish reliable long-term efficacy or safety. Evidence is strongest for transient erectile responses and preliminary pigmentation changes. Claims involving longevity, skin rejuvenation, sustained libido improvement or clinically meaningful fat loss are unsupported by the cited research. The evidence includes early clinical trials, mechanistic research and case reports. It does not establish a validated consumer protocol or quantify uncommon adverse events.

Human studies

The pigmentation pilot included three healthy men; two showed increased pigmentation after the dosing period, PMID 8637402. A crossover trial in ten men with psychogenic erectile dysfunction reported erections in eight participants and longer objectively measured rigidity than placebo, PMID 9679884. Another crossover trial enrolled ten men with organic erectile risk factors, PMID 11018622. Erections were reported after 12 of 19 MT-2 administrations versus 1 of 21 placebo administrations. These administration counts are not independent patient counts. Sexual desire increased on a questionnaire in the latter study. This acute questionnaire result does not establish durable libido improvement, effectiveness in healthy adults, or effectiveness in women.

Preclinical studies

Rat experiments support central and peripheral neural involvement in erection, with effects depending on administration route and experimental conditions, PMID 16360286. These studies help identify pathways but cannot establish a human dose, an exposure ceiling or a useful peptide combination. Anesthetized animal experiments also cannot establish patient satisfaction, sustained sexual-function improvement or tolerability during repeated human exposure. The cited animal findings do not resolve the long-term consequences of melanocyte stimulation.

Safety & limitations

Reported effects

Early trials reported nausea, yawning, stretching, reduced appetite, spontaneous erections, sleepiness and fatigue, PMIDs 8637402, 9679884 and 11018622. Flushing, headache and vomiting also appear in the TGA warning cited under regulatory status. Pigmentation changes can involve freckles and existing moles rather than an even cosmetic change. The small study populations cannot provide reliable adverse-event rates for widespread or prolonged use.

Serious safety signals

Ischemic priapism requiring surgery is documented in PMID 33460908. Renal infarction and previous reports of rhabdomyolysis or renal failure are discussed in PMID 31953620. A melanoma case followed MT-2 exposure together with sunbed use, PMID 24355990. These reports identify safety signals; they do not establish incidence or prove causation. Concurrent UV exposure and uncertain product composition complicate interpretation. Conversely, uncertain causation does not establish that repeated exposure lacks these risks.

Contraindications & cautions

There is no approved MT-2 contraindication list. Risk-based concerns include previous melanoma, suspicious or changing pigmented lesions, strong familial melanoma risk, previous priapism, or conditions predisposing to priapism. Significant kidney disease and unstable cardiovascular disease also warrant particular concern. Pregnancy, breastfeeding and pediatric exposure lack adequate safety evidence. These are precautionary clinical considerations, not trial-validated exclusions or label-derived rules. Their absence does not establish eligibility for use.

When medical assessment is needed

An erection lasting four hours requires emergency assessment, sooner if painful. Severe flank pain, markedly reduced urine, or dark urine with muscle pain warrant urgent care. Chest pain, fainting, severe headache, or signs of a severe allergic reaction also warrant urgent assessment. New or changing skin lesions require prompt clinical assessment. Further exposure should stop while significant adverse effects are evaluated.

Understanding product quality

A certificate of analysis requires separate interpretation of identity, measured peptide content, chromatographic purity, related impurities, sterility and bacterial endotoxins. A high HPLC purity percentage alone does not establish the labeled mass, microbiological quality or clinical suitability. Batch matching, laboratory traceability, methods and testing dates determine what a document can substantiate. Tests not performed cannot be inferred from a purity result. Documentation cannot convert an unapproved substance into an approved medicine. Research-use labeling does not establish safety for human exposure.
