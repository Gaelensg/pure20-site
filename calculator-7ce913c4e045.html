How the Peptide Calculator Works

A peptide calculator connects three values that are easy to confuse: mass in the vial, liquid volume after reconstitution, and the amount represented by each syringe marking. Peptides are short chains of amino acids, and modern peptide research spans approved medicines, clinical candidates, and laboratory-only compounds.[1][2] The calculator does not choose a dose. It performs the arithmetic after a target amount has been supplied from an appropriate research protocol.

The calculation happens in two stages. First, the vial amount is divided by the diluent volume to determine concentration. Second, the target peptide dose is divided by that concentration to determine the volume to draw. On a standard U-100 insulin syringe, 100 units equals 1 mL, so 1 syringe unit represents 0.01 mL.[8] These are volume markings, not peptide International Units (IU).

How to Use the Free Peptide Calculator

The tool is designed as a simple two-card workflow. Use the exact units printed on the vial and in the research protocol, then verify the result independently.

  1. Enter the peptide amount. Type the total amount of peptide in the vial, such as 5 mg or 10 mg. This value is the vial strength and does not change when water is added.
  2. Enter the water volume. Add the planned volume of bacteriostatic water or other compatible diluent in mL. The peptide reconstitution calculator immediately displays mg/mL, mcg per unit, and mcg per 10 units.
  3. Enter the target dose. Type the amount per administration and select mcg or mg. The calculator converts milligrams to micrograms when needed.
  4. Select the syringe barrel. Choose a 30-unit (0.3 mL), 50-unit (0.5 mL), or 100-unit (1 mL) U-100 syringe. The unit result stays the same, while the visible graduation spacing changes.
  5. Read and verify the result. Review the calculated mL, units to draw, full doses per vial, and the illustrated syringe mark. If an optional frequency is selected, the vial-duration estimate is arithmetic only.

Changing the volume of bacteriostatic water changes concentration and draw size, but it does not change the total amount of peptide in the vial or the selected peptide dose.

Peptide Reconstitution Calculator: From Powder to Concentration

Peptide reconstitution is the process of dissolving a lyophilized peptide powder in a compatible liquid so the finished solution has a known concentration. Lyophilization, or freeze-drying, is widely used to improve the storage stability of labile biological molecules, but formulation, container, temperature, and handling still determine actual stability.[3][4] A calculator cannot determine diluent compatibility or a beyond-use date.

Use the diluent named by the product, supplier, laboratory, or validated protocol. When a procedure calls for bacteriostatic water, the labeled product commonly contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol and is supplied in a multi-dose container.[5] USP peptide guidance also emphasizes following the procedure-specific container, diluent, and reconstitution requirements.[12]

Bacteriostatic Water Volume and Concentration

The volume of bacteriostatic water controls how concentrated the reconstituted solution becomes. A 5 mg vial reconstituted with 1 mL has a concentration of 5 mg/mL. The same 5 mg vial reconstituted with 2 mL has a concentration of 2.5 mg/mL. Adding more water creates a larger volume to draw for the same peptide dose; adding less water creates a smaller draw.

Benzyl alcohol is used as an antimicrobial preservative in some multi-dose protein and peptide formulations, but preservative compatibility is formulation-specific.[6] Research has also shown that benzyl alcohol can increase aggregation in some reconstituted protein formulations under particular conditions.[7] Therefore, this bac water calculator does not imply that bacteriostatic water is suitable for every peptide.

How to Calculate Peptide Dosage

The peptide dosage calculation uses four short equations:

  1. Concentration (mg/mL) = peptide amount in mg ÷ final liquid volume in mL.
  2. Target dose (mg) = target dose in mcg ÷ 1,000.
  3. Volume to draw (mL) = target dose in mg ÷ concentration in mg/mL.
  4. U-100 syringe units = volume to draw in mL × 100.

For example, a 250 mcg dose equals 0.25 mg. If the concentration is 2.5 mg/mL, then 0.25 mg ÷ 2.5 mg/mL = 0.1 mL. On a U-100 syringe, 0.1 mL equals 10 units. The calculator performs the same arithmetic for any vial amount, water volume, and desired dose entered above.

Peptide Calculator Results: Syringe Units and Barrel Size

U-100 identifies a volumetric scale originally designed for insulin at 100 units per mL.[8] When the scale is used for a research calculation, the number of units tells you the liquid volume only. It does not describe peptide mass until the reconstitution concentration is known.

A 5-unit draw is 0.05 mL, 10 units is 0.10 mL, 20 units is 0.20 mL, and 50 units is 0.50 mL. The same unit number represents the same volume on 30-, 50-, and 100-unit U-100 barrels. Smaller barrels usually provide more space between low-volume markings, which can make a small draw easier to read.

Worked Example: 5 mg Vial Reconstituted With 2 mL

Start with a 5 mg peptide vial and 2 mL of bacteriostatic water. The concentration is 5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL, which is also 2,500 mcg/mL. One U-100 syringe unit is 0.01 mL, so each unit contains 25 mcg at this concentration.

For a 250 mcg research amount, the volume to draw is 250 mcg ÷ 2,500 mcg/mL = 0.10 mL. That equals 10 units on a U-100 syringe. The vial contains 5,000 mcg total, so it holds 20 complete 250 mcg calculation doses before accounting for handling loss or dead space.

If the same 5 mg vial were reconstituted with 1 mL, the concentration would be 5 mg/mL and the same 250 mcg amount would equal 5 units. With 3 mL, the concentration would be approximately 1.67 mg/mL and the calculated draw would be about 15 units. The peptide amount stays fixed; only concentration and volume to draw change.

Accuracy Limits for Small Syringe Draws

A mathematically correct result can still be difficult to measure physically. Research on small-volume syringe measurement found more frequent unacceptable error when the measured volume was less than 20% of the syringe's labeled capacity.[9] A separate low-dose study found the largest percentage error at a 1-unit syringe dose compared with larger draws.[10]

The visual meter and barrel recommendation help identify when a draw is very small or exceeds the selected syringe. They do not validate a particular syringe, technique, compound, route, or protocol. Always use the actual graduation marks on the selected device and independently confirm the input values.

Peptide Frequently Asked Questions

What information do I need to use a peptide calculator?

You need the total peptide amount in the vial, the final liquid volume after reconstitution, and the target amount per administration. The calculator accepts vial mass in mg, water volume in mL, and target dose in either mcg or mg.

How do I calculate peptide dosage in syringe units?

Divide the desired dose by the concentration to get mL, then multiply the mL by 100 for a U-100 syringe. A 250 mcg dose from a 2.5 mg/mL solution equals 0.10 mL, or 10 units on a U-100 syringe.

Does adding more water change the peptide dose?

No. Adding more water lowers the concentration and increases the volume needed to reach the same dose. It does not change the peptide amount in the vial or the target dose entered into the calculator.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add to a peptide vial?

The calculator can compare 1 mL, 2 mL, 3 mL, or another water volume, but it cannot select the proper diluent or volume for a compound. Use product-specific instructions or a validated research procedure, then enter that volume to calculate concentration and syringe units.

Are U-100 syringe units the same as peptide IU?

No. U-100 syringe units are volume markings: 100 units equals 1 mL. International Units (IU) describe biological activity for certain substances and cannot be converted to peptide mass without a substance-specific standard.

What syringe size is best for a small calculated draw?

Use the smallest compatible barrel that comfortably contains the full draw and has markings that match the required precision. The calculator compares 30-, 50-, and 100-unit U-100 barrels and flags values that cannot be represented cleanly.

How should a reconstituted peptide be stored?

Storage temperature, light protection, freeze-thaw limits, and discard timing depend on the specific peptide and formulation. Follow the product label, supplier stability documentation, or validated laboratory procedure. Do not infer a universal storage period from vial strength, water type, or this calculator. General multi-dose vial guidance also defers to a manufacturer's shorter product-specific limit.[11]

Can sterile water replace bacteriostatic water in the calculation?

The concentration math is the same for any compatible diluent because it uses liquid volume. Compatibility and handling are not the same. Sterile Water for Injection has no antimicrobial preservative, while Bacteriostatic Water for Injection commonly contains benzyl alcohol. Use only the diluent specified for the product or validated procedure.

How do I convert mcg to mL?

Mass cannot be converted to volume without a concentration. First convert the vial and water inputs into mcg/mL. Then divide the target mcg by that concentration to obtain mL. The peptide reconstitution calculator completes both steps automatically.
