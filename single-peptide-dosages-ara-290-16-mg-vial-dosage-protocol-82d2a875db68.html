Ara-290 Dosage Chart

Dosing & Reconstitution Guide

Educational guide for reconstitution and daily dosing

Standard / Gradual Approach (2.0 mL = 8 mg/mL)

Week Daily Dose Units (per injection) (mL)
Week 1 2 mg (2,000 mcg) 25 units (0.25 mL)
Weeks 2–8 (or up to 16) 4 mg (4,000 mcg) 50 units (0.50 mL)

Frequency: Inject once daily subcutaneously. Clinical studies used 4 mg/day as the target therapeutic dose, with no additional benefit observed at 8 mg[3][6]. Starting at 2 mg for the first week allows assessment of individual tolerance before reaching the maintenance dose.

Reconstitution Steps

  1. Draw 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water with a sterile syringe.
  2. Inject slowly down the vial wall; avoid foaming.
  3. Gently swirl or roll until fully dissolved (do not shake vigorously).
  4. Label with reconstitution date and refrigerate at 2–8 °C (35.6–46.4 °F), protected from light.

Important: This guide is for educational purposes only and is not medical advice. For research use only. Not for human consumption.

Ara-290 Vial and Research Context

  • Reconstitute: Add 2.0 mL bacteriostatic water → 8 mg/mL concentration.
  • Target dose: 4 mg once daily subcutaneously (after initial 2 mg titration).
  • Easy measuring: At 8 mg/mL, 1 unit = 0.01 mL = 80 mcg on a U-100 insulin syringe.
  • Storage: Lyophilized: refrigerate at 2–8 °C (35.6–46.4 °F) or freeze at −20 °C (−4 °F); reconstituted: refrigerate and follow a validated product-specific use-by period.

ARA-290, also called cibinetide, is an investigational 11-amino-acid peptide derived from erythropoietin. It was engineered to activate the innate repair receptor without the red-blood-cell-stimulating activity associated with erythropoietin.[1][2] Human research has examined small-fiber neuropathy in type 2 diabetes and sarcoidosis, but ARA-290 is not FDA-approved and the evidence does not establish a general treatment recommendation.[3][4][10]

Protocol Overview

This 16 mg protocol translates published ARA-290 research amounts into vial concentration, U-100 syringe volume, and supply calculations while keeping the evidence limits visible.

  • Compound: ARA-290, also known as cibinetide, is an investigational erythropoietin-derived peptide.[1]
  • Evidence anchor: A phase 2 study evaluated 4 mg subcutaneously once daily for 28 days in participants with type 2 diabetes and painful neuropathy.[3]
  • 16 mg calculation: 2.0 mL yields 8 mg/mL; 4 mg equals 50 U-100 units, or 0.50 mL.
  • Scope: The calculation does not validate the identity, purity, sterility, or equivalence of a commercial research vial.
  • Status: ARA-290 is not FDA-approved. Human findings remain investigational and condition-specific.[10]
06

Human Research Schedules

Published studies used different routes, populations, and schedules. They should not be blended into one generalized regimen.

  • Type 2 diabetes with painful neuropathy: 4 mg subcutaneously once daily for 28 days.[3]
  • Sarcoidosis pilot: 2 mg intravenously three times weekly for four weeks.[5]
  • Sarcoidosis dose-ranging study: 1 mg, 4 mg, or 8 mg subcutaneously for 28 consecutive days.[6]
  • Study design: The cited 4 mg daily phase 2 study used the same amount throughout its 28-day treatment period.[3]

The diabetes study record and published report document the 4 mg, 28-day schedule, while the sarcoidosis review, published outcomes, and European trial record describe separate condition-specific designs and endpoints.[7][11][18][19][20]

Storage Instructions

Storage conditions and use-by periods depend on formulation, container, sterility controls, and validated product data.

  • Follow the exact product label and supplier documentation for the unopened lyophilized vial.
  • After reconstitution, refrigerate only when supported by the product’s validated instructions.
  • Protect the vial from light and avoid repeated temperature cycling.
  • Do not use a vial that is cloudy, discolored, damaged, or contains unexpected particles.

General references on lyophilized formulations and compounding explain why stability and beyond-use dating must be formulation-specific; neither source establishes a product-specific shelf life.[21][22]

08

What the 16 mg Vial Changes

The vial strength changes concentration, syringe markings, and whole-vial supply totals. It does not change what a clinical study administered. With 2.0 mL added to a 16 mg vial, the concentration is 8 mg/mL.

  • 1 U-100 unit: 0.01 mL × 8 mg/mL = 0.08 mg, or 80 mcg.
  • 2 mg calculation: 2 mg ÷ 8 mg/mL = 0.25 mL, or 25 units.
  • 4 mg calculation: 4 mg ÷ 8 mg/mL = 0.50 mL, or 50 units.
  • Whole-vial yield: A 16 mg vial contains four complete 4 mg amounts. That arithmetic does not establish a stability period or authorize any use beyond verified product instructions.

U-100 syringe units are volume markings. They are not peptide international units and should never be substituted for mass in milligrams or micrograms.

09

Important Calculation Notes

  • Do not confuse a 16 mg vial strength with a 16 mg amount per administration.
  • Recalculate units whenever vial mass or final liquid volume changes.
  • Round supply quantities up to whole vials and whole packages. Never round the required mass down.
  • Keep intravenous and subcutaneous research schedules separate. Route changes alter interpretation and cannot be made by unit conversion alone.
  • Use a new sterile syringe for every administration and place used sharps in an approved sharps container.
10

How ARA-290 Works

ARA-290 is a small, 11-amino-acid peptide modeled on the three-dimensional structure of erythropoietin. It was designed to activate tissue-protective signaling without activating the classical erythropoietin pathway responsible for red blood cell production.[1][2] The proposed target is the innate repair receptor, described as a receptor complex involving the erythropoietin receptor and the beta-common receptor CD131.[8]

Preclinical work has examined anti-inflammatory signaling, neuropathic pain, and nerve-injury models. Human clinical trials then evaluated ARA-290 in small-fiber neuropathy associated with type 2 diabetes and sarcoidosis.[3][4] Mechanistic plausibility and early clinical findings are not the same as proven clinical effectiveness. Longer and larger independent trials are needed.

Additional tissue-protection and neuropathy reviews discuss the injury-response context and innate repair receptor rationale, while a preclinical study examined TRPV1-related pain signaling.[9][16][17][24]

11

Human Findings, Safety, and Uncertainty

The available human evidence is preliminary and population-specific.

  • Type 2 diabetes with painful neuropathy: A 28-day phase 2 trial reported changes in neuropathic symptoms, metabolic measures, and corneal nerve fiber density after 4 mg subcutaneous ARA-290 daily.[3]
  • Sarcoidosis-associated small-fiber neuropathy: Small randomized studies reported changes in symptoms and corneal or cutaneous nerve-fiber measures.[4][5]
  • Hematologic design: ARA-290 was engineered to separate tissue-protective signaling from erythropoietin’s red-blood-cell-stimulating activity.[1][2]
  • Short-term tolerability: The cited trials did not identify a clear short-term safety signal at the studied schedules, but their sample sizes and durations cannot establish long-term safety.[3][5]
  • Clinical uncertainty: The ERS guideline found insufficient evidence to support a treatment recommendation for sarcoidosis-related small-fiber neuropathy.[10]

Corneal nerve-fiber outcomes were also reported in ophthalmic research involving sarcoidosis-associated small-fiber neuropathy.[25]

12

Clinical Evidence and Its Limits

ARA-290 has human research, but the evidence base is still limited. The published trials were small, short, and focused on specific neuropathy populations. The 2014 phase 2 study in type 2 diabetes reported changes in metabolic measures, neuropathic symptoms, and corneal nerve fiber density after 28 days, but the authors called for continued clinical evaluation.[3]

In sarcoidosis, exploratory studies reported changes in symptoms and corneal nerve measures.[4][5] Those findings do not prove that ARA-290 is effective for other causes of neuropathy, tissue injury, or pain. European Respiratory Society guidelines later concluded that evidence for treating sarcoidosis-related small-fiber neuropathy was insufficient to support a recommendation.[10]

Clinical-trial material was manufactured and administered under controlled research conditions. A third-party 16 mg vial cannot be assumed to match those formulations in identity, purity, sterility, excipients, bioavailability, or stability.

13

Subcutaneous Technique Context

The primary calculation on this page follows the subcutaneous route used in the cited phase 2 study. General technique references describe site selection and injection mechanics, but they do not validate ARA-290 or a specific research product.[12][13]

  • Use a new sterile syringe and needle for every administration.
  • Clean the vial stopper and selected injection site with alcohol and allow both to dry.
  • Use an appropriate subcutaneous site and rotate sites systematically.
  • Keep the U-100 volume conversion separate from the clinical decision about route, amount, and schedule.
  • Place used needles immediately in an approved sharps container.

General subcutaneous-delivery reviews and patient guidance from Cleveland Clinic and Johns Hopkins, together with CDC route guidance, describe technique considerations; these sources do not validate ARA-290 or a specific research product.[14][15][23][26][27]

ARA-290 16 mg Vial FAQ

How many U-100 units equal 4 mg from this 16 mg vial?

With 2.0 mL added, the concentration is 8 mg/mL. Four milligrams requires 0.50 mL, which is 50 units on a U-100 syringe.

How many U-100 units equal 2 mg?

At 8 mg/mL, 2 mg requires 0.25 mL, or 25 U-100 units. This is a mathematical conversion, not a recommendation to administer 2 mg.

How many 4 mg amounts are in a 16 mg vial?

The vial contains four complete 4 mg amounts. That arithmetic does not establish how long the reconstituted vial remains usable.

Does a 16 mg vial change the amount used in the human studies?

No. Vial strength changes concentration and supply math. It does not change the study protocol. The main phase 2 study cited on this page evaluated 4 mg subcutaneously once daily for 28 days.[3]

Is ARA-290 FDA-approved?

No. ARA-290, or cibinetide, remains investigational. Human trials do not constitute regulatory approval or a general treatment recommendation.
