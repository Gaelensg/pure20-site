Retatrutide Quick Reference (50 mg Vial)

Vial contents
50 mg Retatrutide
Final volume
2.5 mL
Concentration
20 mg/mL
One U-100 unit
200 mcg in 0.01 mL

Research context: Retatrutide is an investigational triple-receptor agonist studied in adults with obesity and in adults with type 2 diabetes. Published trials used once-weekly subcutaneous administration with protocol-defined dose escalation; the 50 mg vial size is a calculation format, not a distinct clinical regimen.[1][3][5][6]

Reconstitution Steps

  1. Clean the vial stopper and gather the sterile supplies required by the research procedure.
  2. Add 2.5 mL bacteriostatic water slowly down the vial wall.
  3. Gently swirl or roll until dissolved. Do not shake.
  4. Label the vial 20 mg/mL with the preparation date and follow the documented refrigerated storage period.

Research reference only: Phase 2 and phase 3 programs used protocol-defined once-weekly schedules and gradual escalation. The rows above translate those studied amounts into 20 mg/mL volume markings; they are not a prescription or a universal maintenance schedule.[1][2][3][5][6][10][12]

Retatrutide Vial and Research Context

  • Reconstitute: Add 2.5 mL of bacteriostatic water to produce 20 mg/mL.
  • U-100 conversion: At 20 mg/mL, 1 unit = 0.01 mL = 200 mcg.
  • Trial schedule: Published programs evaluated once-weekly subcutaneous dosing with staged escalation.[1][2][3][5]
  • Chart amounts: The tables translate 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, and 12 mg into volume markings without creating a recommendation.
  • 50 mg vial yield: One vial contains 25 calculations at 2 mg, 12.5 at 4 mg, 6.25 at 8 mg, or about 4.17 at 12 mg before preparation limits are considered.
  • Evidence boundary: Retatrutide remains investigational. These calculations organize published research schedules and do not establish individualized dosing, safety, or suitability.[1][3][5][6]
05

Protocol Overview

Key facts for interpreting the 50 mg calculation page.

  • Compound: Retatrutide, an investigational GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptor agonist.[4][7][8]
  • Route and frequency in trials: Subcutaneous injection once weekly.[1][3][5][6]
  • Prepared concentration: 50 mg ÷ 2.5 mL = 20 mg/mL.
  • U-100 conversion: 1 unit = 0.01 mL = 0.2 mg (200 mcg).
  • Studied targets: Published programs included 4 mg, 8 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg targets after protocol-defined starting doses and escalation.[1][2][3][5]
06

Dosing Protocol

How to distinguish amount, escalation, and vial-strength math.

  • Amount per administration: Each chart row is one once-weekly amount, not a daily total.[1][3][5]
  • Starting dose: Some trial arms began at 2 mg before increasing to higher assigned targets.[1][2][3][5][10][12]
  • Step-up schedule: Escalation was part of the phase 2 and phase 3 clinical trials and differed by trial; the two tables should not be combined.[1][2][3][5][6]
  • Vial strength: A 50 mg vial changes concentration, unit markings, theoretical yield, and supplies—not the evidence-based dose range.
  • Research record: Track the assigned protocol, week, amount, calculated units, mL, lot, date, and injection site.
07

Storage Instructions

Preparation records and stability limits remain product specific.

  • Label the vial: Record compound, 20 mg/mL concentration, preparation date, and lot or source identifier.
  • Follow verified instructions: Storage temperature, light protection, and discard timing must come from the exact product or study documentation.
  • Do not infer a universal use-by period: Vial strength alone does not establish post-reconstitution stability.
  • Limit temperature cycling: Protein and peptide stability can be affected by repeated freeze-thaw exposure.[16]
  • Maintain sterile technique: Use a new sterile syringe and appropriate sharps disposal practices.[13][14][15]

  • 4 mg: 0.20 mL = 20 units; a 50 mg vial contains 12.5 theoretical 4 mg portions.
  • 6 mg: 0.30 mL = 30 units; a 50 mg vial contains about 8.33 theoretical 6 mg portions.
  • 8 mg: 0.40 mL = 40 units; a 50 mg vial contains 6.25 theoretical 8 mg portions.
  • 9 mg: 0.45 mL = 45 units; a 50 mg vial contains about 5.56 theoretical 9 mg portions.
  • 12 mg: 0.60 mL = 60 units; a 50 mg vial contains about 4.17 theoretical 12 mg portions.
  • Theoretical yield does not account for dead space, handling loss, sterility limits, or the product-specific discard date.

Retatrutide Trial Targets and Escalation

Retatrutide dosage research used assigned study arms rather than one universal schedule. The evidence must be read by population, target dose, starting dose, and escalation design.

  • The phase 2 obesity trial evaluated once-weekly 1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, and 12 mg groups; several higher-target arms began at 2 mg or 4 mg and escalated.[1][6][10]
  • The retatrutide dose in each clinical trial followed a preset dosing schedule. In the phase 2 trial, some participants started at 2 mg once weekly before dose increases every 4 weeks toward an assigned 4 mg, 8 mg, or 12 mg target; the published trials were sponsored by Eli Lilly.[1][5][10][12]
  • The phase 2 type 2 diabetes trial evaluated 0.5 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, and 12 mg retatrutide groups, with multiple escalation designs.[5][12]
  • The TRIUMPH phase 3 program specified target doses and escalation schedules for obesity-related research.[2]
  • TRANSCEND-T2D-1 evaluated 4 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg target doses in adults with type 2 diabetes inadequately controlled with diet and exercise.[3]
  • A starting dose, step-up schedule, and highest studied target are trial variables; they should not be restated as individualized instructions.
10

Triple-Receptor Research Context

Retatrutide was engineered as a single peptide with agonist activity at three metabolic hormone receptors.

  • The molecule activates glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP), glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), and glucagon receptors.[4]
  • The triple agonist design is being studied for integrated effects on appetite, glucose regulation, energy balance, and body weight.[4][7][8]
  • Multi-receptor activity does not make results interchangeable with semaglutide, tirzepatide, or other incretin-based compounds; each evidence base and trial schedule must be evaluated separately.[7][8][9]
  • Retatrutide remains investigational, and ongoing research continues to define efficacy, safety, and appropriate study populations.[2][3][6]
11

Clinical Findings by Study Population

Results should be tied to the population and endpoint studied rather than generalized from the vial size.

  • In adults with obesity, the phase 2 trial reported dose-dependent body-weight changes through 48 weeks across once-weekly study arms.[1]
  • In adults with type 2 diabetes, the phase 2 trial examined glycated hemoglobin and body-weight outcomes through 24 and 36 weeks.[5]
  • The phase 3 TRANSCEND-T2D-1 trial evaluated 4 mg, 9 mg, and 12 mg targets and reported glycemic and body-weight outcomes in its enrolled population.[3]
  • A phase 2a substudy in participants with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease evaluated changes in liver fat at 24 weeks.[11]
  • These findings do not validate a 50 mg vial, a compounded product, or any unsupervised protocol; the vial calculation is separate from clinical evidence.
12

Safety Findings and Evidence Limits

Safety interpretation depends on the studied population, starting dose, escalation rate, target dose, and duration.

  • Retatrutide side effects: Gastrointestinal events—including nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation—were commonly reported in phase 2 research and varied across dose and escalation groups.[1][5][10][12]
  • In the obesity phase 2 trial, dose-dependent heart-rate increases peaked during the study and later declined.[1]
  • Trial protocols used eligibility criteria, clinical monitoring, and prespecified escalation; a vial-math page cannot reproduce those safeguards.[1][3][5][6]
  • The evidence does not establish a general maintenance dose or maximum dose for an individual outside a research protocol.
  • Preparation quality and repeated temperature changes can also affect peptide stability, so use product-specific storage instructions.[16]
13

Research Tracking Variables

A clear log helps separate concentration math from the assigned protocol and supports reproducible research records.

  • Record the vial strength, diluent volume, calculated concentration, lot, preparation date, and verified storage conditions.
  • For each administration, record the protocol week, assigned amount, calculated mL, U-100 units, date, and injection site.
  • Rotate subcutaneous sites and use clean equipment according to the governing study procedure.[13][14][15]
  • Document deviations, missed observations, adverse events, and changes directed by the study investigator rather than improvising a catch-up schedule.
  • Keep supplier pages and product labels separate from scientific evidence; use the cited literature to interpret trial claims.

Frequently Asked Questions About 50 mg Retatrutide

Common calculation questions about this vial strength can be answered without turning research arithmetic into medical advice.

  • What concentration results from adding 2.5 mL? A 50 mg vial divided by 2.5 mL produces 20 mg/mL.
  • How many mcg are in one U-100 unit? One unit is 0.01 mL, so at 20 mg/mL it contains 0.2 mg, or 200 mcg.
  • Does a 50 mg vial change the retatrutide dosage schedule? No. Vial strength changes the volume calculation and theoretical yield, while the cited trials define their own assigned amounts and escalation.[1][2][3][5]
  • What was the highest studied weekly target in the cited programs? The cited phase 2 and phase 3 sources include 12 mg once weekly as a target, but that is a trial parameter—not a recommendation.[1][2][3][5][6]
  • Was a gradual dose escalation used? Yes. In several phase 2 trial arms, participants started at 2 mg once weekly before dose increases every 4 weeks according to the assigned study protocol.[1][5][10][12]
  • Is retatrutide approved for routine use? No. Retatrutide remains investigational; this page is an educational calculation reference for published research.[2][3][6]
